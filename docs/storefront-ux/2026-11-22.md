## Sourced observations (observed pages, not copied layouts)
- [Christianbook — About Bibles](https://www.christianbook.com/page/bibles/about-bibles): offers separate guidance for translations, bindings, Bible types, and large print, showing the need to separate textual choice from edition attributes.
- [Christianbook — Bible Store](https://www.christianbook.com/page/bibles?event=FOOT): separately groups Bibles by translation and by type, with a translation guide.
- [Baymard — Mobile Product Lists](https://baymard.com/mcommerce-usability/benchmark/mobile-page-types/product-list): highlights the tension between sufficient product information and limited mobile screen width.
- [Baymard — Quick Views for Visually Driven Products](https://baymard.com/research-articles/mobile-desktop-quick-views): explicitly cautions against quick-view patterns for spec-driven products; avoid forcing edition specifications into a superficial quick view.
No new verified observations are claimed for Amazon, Apple, Nike, Crossway or NN/g today.

## UX problems prioritized
1. A translation comparison does not itself tell a buyer how to choose a specific printed edition; customers could conflate translation, print size and binding.
2. Generic browse CTA can imply available editions without a verified catalog. This is a design hypothesis, not a measured conversion problem.
3. Mobile two-column text remains dense and requires visual and assistive-technology checks.

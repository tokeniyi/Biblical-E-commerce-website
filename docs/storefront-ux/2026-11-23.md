## Sourced research observations
- [Christianbook — Bible Translations](https://www.christianbook.com/page/bibles/translations): places ESV among word-for-word translations and NIV in the middle of the translation-method spectrum; the previous design's “NIV generally more thought-for-thought” overstates that distinction.
- [Christianbook — About Translations](https://www.christianbook.com/page/bibles/about-bibles/about-translations): provides passage-based comparison options, suggesting sample reading as a useful step before edition selection.
- [Baymard — Product spec sheet UX](https://baymard.com/blog/product-spec-sheet-ux-design): recommends consistent attribute labels and units across products; apply to future binding, dimensions, and print-size comparisons.
- [Baymard — Mobile product lists](https://baymard.com/mcommerce-usability/benchmark/mobile-page-types/product-list): identifies the tradeoff between relevant product details and limited mobile width.

No new claims are made about Amazon, Apple, Nike, Crossway or NN/g.

## Prioritized user problems
1. The NIV descriptor could mislead customers about its translation approach. Priority: factual accuracy in a comparison meant to inform purchasing.
2. The previous duplicated prompt focused on edition details before helping users assess the translated text. Priority: a clearer sequence of decisions.
3. Dense two-column mobile layout remains an unvalidated readability risk.
